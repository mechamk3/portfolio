## Portfolio structure
- Site copy lives in `src/content/{en,fa}.ts` (+ `hris/` for the case study); pages only read from there, so keep the dictionaries free of keys no page renders.
- Every page sits under `src/routes/$locale.*`; `/` redirects to the default locale (fa) and `__root` sets `lang`/`dir` from the locale param.
- The shared ThemeProvider defaults to dark, restores the visitor's local theme preference after hydration, and controls the root dark class; all colors use global semantic tokens with light overrides.
- Shared project artwork lives in ProjectVisual and uses real screenshots or domain symbols, so home and project-list cards stay visually consistent without fabricated product data.
- The shared Backdrop supplies a quiet, token-based flat canvas to every locale page; keep decoration centralized rather than layering it into sections.
- The footer AccentPicker (ThemeProvider `ACCENTS`, persisted in localStorage, applied as `data-accent` on `<html>`) re-themes the whole site: every palette color derives from `--h/--hs/--hg/--k` in styles.css, so never hardcode hues in components; `mono` sets `--k: 0`. Add a new accent as an `ACCENTS` entry, a `[data-accent]` rule and `footer.colors` copy in both dictionaries.
- Default palette is "plum ink & lilac mist" with one orchid signal: dark is plum-ink, light is cool lilac-grey, never pure black/white. Avoid cream/navy/dot-grid (the old master look). Base colors and semantic roles are defined centrally in global CSS; section backgrounds are flat full-width bands that alternate `--band-a`/`--band-b` (footer matches the navbar canvas), never gradients, grids, dots or hatching. Success, warning, and danger retain distinct semantic hues independently of brand accents.
